Claim FY25-26 ITC Before 30 Nov 2026: Last-Date Guide

If you bought something for your business in FY 2025-26 (April 2025 to March 2026) and have not claimed the GST input tax credit (ITC) yet, there is a hard stop coming: 30 November 2026.

Tax ki tension kam: jo GST pehle bhara, woh wapas claim karo, deadline se pehle.

The rule in one line

Under Section 16(4) of the CGST Act, ITC on an invoice or debit note for a financial year cannot be taken after 30 November following the end of that year, or after you file the annual return (GSTR-9) for that year, whichever is earlier.

For FY 2025-26, that means 30 November 2026, or earlier if you file GSTR-9 before then.

Which return is your "last chance"?

  • Monthly filer: GSTR-3B for October 2026, normally due 20 November 2026, is the last regular return inside the window. Filing it after 30 Nov does not save the claim.
  • QRMP filer: GSTR-3B for Jul–Sep 2026 (due 22/24 Oct 2026) is your last scheduled return before the cut-off. The Oct–Dec return is due in January, after the deadline.

So for most small businesses the practical deadline is this month's or next month's 3B, not 30 November itself.

Rule 37A: reversal if your supplier did not file

Under CGST Rule 37A, if the supplier has not filed GSTR-3B for the period by 30 September after the financial year ends, reverse that ITC in a return filed on or before 30 November of that year. Re-claim it once the supplier files (Notif. 26/2022-CT).

Your ITC clean-up checklist

  1. Download GSTR-2B for every month of FY 2025-26 (and check any "ITC at risk" flags on the portal).
  2. Match against your purchase register. List invoices that show in 2B but you never claimed, and the other way round.
  3. Chase missing suppliers. If an invoice is not in your 2B, ask the supplier to report it in GSTR-1 now. Once 30 Nov passes, it is too late.
  4. Check the conditions in Section 16(2): you hold the invoice, you received the goods or service, the supplier has paid the tax, and you have filed your return. If you don't pay the supplier (value + tax) within 180 days of the invoice date, add that ITC to your output tax liability with interest; you can re-claim it on payment (second proviso to s.16(2) CGST Act; Rule 37).
  5. Leave out blocked credits (Section 17(5) items, such as most food or personal-use purchases).
  6. Reverse under Rule 37A where suppliers have not filed.
  7. Claim in your next GSTR-3B, before 30 Nov 2026.
  8. Do not file GSTR-9 early if you still have ITC to clean up. Filing it closes the window.

How much is at stake?

Say a freelancer bought a ₹1,18,000 laptop (₹1,00,000 + ₹18,000 GST) in February 2026 for work and never claimed the credit. That is ₹18,000 of tax already paid that they can use against GST payable, if it is claimed in a return filed by 30 Nov 2026. After that, it is gone. (Illustrative. Eligibility depends on business use and the other conditions.)

Worked example: the reconciliation that finds money

Anil, a monthly filer, compares his FY 2025-26 purchase register with GSTR-2B:

Item Amount of GST
In 2B, claimed ₹1,80,000
In 2B, never claimed ₹14,000
In register, not in 2B (supplier did not report) ₹6,000
Supplier did not file 3B (Rule 37A) ₹4,000

His action list: - Claim the ₹14,000 in his October 2026 GSTR-3B, filed by 30 Nov. - Chase the supplier on the ₹6,000 right away. - Reverse the ₹4,000 under Rule 37A, and re-claim it if the supplier files later.

Common ITC mistakes

  • Assuming the deadline is 20 Nov or 31 Dec. It is the earlier of 30 Nov or the date you file GSTR-9.
  • Claiming credit on personal purchases.
  • Not following up with suppliers until after the deadline.
  • Filing GSTR-9 early with unclaimed credit still outstanding.

A one-week plan to finish before 30 Nov

  • Day 1–2: download every GSTR-2B for FY 2025-26 and export your purchase register.
  • Day 3: match them and list unclaimed, missing and Rule 37A items.
  • Day 4–5: email suppliers about missing invoices.
  • Day 6: prepare the claim and reversal figures for your next GSTR-3B.
  • Day 7: file well before the cut-off.

FAQ

What is the last date to claim ITC for FY 2025-26? 30 November 2026, or the date you file the annual return (GSTR-9) for FY 2025-26, whichever is earlier, under Section 16(4) of the CGST Act.

Which GSTR-3B is the last one to claim FY 2025-26 ITC? For monthly filers, the October 2026 GSTR-3B (normally due 20 November 2026) filed by 30 November. For QRMP filers, the Jul–Sep 2026 GSTR-3B is the last scheduled return before the cut-off.

What is Rule 37A? Rule 37A of the CGST Rules requires a recipient to reverse ITC by 30 November if the supplier has not filed GSTR-3B for that period by 30 September of the next financial year. The ITC can be re-claimed after the supplier files.

Does filing GSTR-9 early affect my ITC claim? Yes. The Section 16(4) window closes at the earlier of 30 November or the date of filing the annual return, so filing GSTR-9 early ends it early.

Can I claim ITC after 30 November 2026 for FY 2025-26? Not under the normal rule in Section 16(4). Missed ITC for FY 2025-26 generally cannot be claimed after that date.
