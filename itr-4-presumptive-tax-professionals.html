ITR-4 and Presumptive Tax for Professionals: Old 44ADA, Now Section 58

If you are a consultant, architect, doctor, engineer or other professional with modest receipts, the presumptive scheme can save you hours of bookkeeping. You declare 50% of your gross receipts as income and file a short return, ITR-4 (Sugam). Here is how it works now that the Income-tax Act, 2025 has replaced the 1961 Act.

Tax ki tension kam: aadhi receipts income, baaki kharcha maana gaya. Simple!

Old section, new section

Income-tax Act, 1961 Income-tax Act, 2025 (from 1 Apr 2026)
Presumptive scheme for professionals Section 44ADA Section 58(2), Table Sl. No. 3
Who counts as a "specified profession" Section 44AA(1) Section 62(4)
Advance tax in one instalment Section 211 Section 408(2)

The Income Tax Department's FAQ on presumptive taxation confirms the scheme now sits in Section 58(2) [Table Sl. No. 3].

Which Act applies depends on the year: - FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27): old Act, Section 44ADA. The ITR-4 notified for AY 2026-27 still refers to 44ADA. - Tax year 2026-27 onwards (April 2026 to March 2027): new Act, Section 58. Rule 164 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026 sets out who can use ITR-4 (Sugam) for tax years from 1 April 2026.

The rules in plain numbers (Section 58(2), Table Sl. No. 3)

  • Who: a resident individual, or a partnership firm (not an LLP), carrying on a specified profession.
  • Limit: gross receipts up to ₹50 lakh, or up to ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are no more than 5% of gross receipts. A cheque or bank draft that is not account payee counts as cash.
  • Income: 50% of gross receipts, or more if you actually earned more.
  • Advance tax: all of it in one instalment by 15 March (Section 408(2)). See our advance tax guide.

Under Section 62(4) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (via Section 58(2) Table S. No. 3), the specified professions are legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, film artist, authorised representative, company secretary, information technology, and any other profession the CBDT notifies. Graphic design and content writing are not on the list, so check before using this scheme. Software or IT work falls under 'information technology profession'. If your work is not a specified profession, this scheme is not for you, though a separate business scheme exists under Section 58(2) Table Sl. No. 1.

Who can file ITR-4 (Sugam)

Under Rule 164 (Income-tax Rules, 2026), ITR-4 is for resident (not RNOR) individuals, HUFs and firms other than LLPs with presumptive income, and total income up to ₹50 lakh. (HUFs can file ITR-4 for presumptive business income, but the professionals' scheme in Table S. No. 3 is only for resident individuals and partnership firms other than LLPs.) You cannot use ITR-4 if, among other things, you: - have total income above ₹50 lakh - are a director in a company - held unlisted equity shares at any time in the year - have assets or income outside India, or signing authority in a foreign account - have agricultural income above ₹5,000 - have brought-forward or carry-forward losses - own more than two house properties

Worked example: a consultant on the scheme

Anita, a technical consultant (a specified profession), tax year 2026-27: - Gross receipts: ₹30,00,000, all by bank transfer - Actual expenses: ₹6,00,000 - Presumptive income: 50% × ₹30,00,000 = ₹15,00,000

She pays tax on ₹15 lakh, even though her actual profit was ₹24 lakh, and she does not need detailed books for the scheme. TDS her clients deducted (10% on professional fees, see our TDS guide) counts against her tax. Add it up with the TDS calculator.

Worked example: the 5% cash test

Vikram, an architect, has gross receipts of ₹62,00,000: - If ₹2,50,000 came in cash (4%), the limit is ₹75 lakh, so he qualifies. - If ₹4,00,000 came in cash (6.5%), the limit is ₹50 lakh, so he does not qualify and needs regular books (and possibly an audit).

Taking payments through banking channels keeps the higher limit open.

When the scheme is a bad deal

If your real expenses are more than half your receipts, say heavy software, equipment and subcontracting costs, paying tax on 50% means paying tax on profit you never made. In that case, normal accounts may give a lower tax. The ITR-4 form for AY 2026-27 notes that declaring less than 50% under 44ADA requires a tax audit and a different ITR. Speak to a CA before you switch.

GST is separate

The presumptive scheme is about income tax only. If your turnover crosses the GST registration threshold, you still have to register and charge GST. See our GST registration threshold guide and the GST rate guide. Most practitioners compute gross receipts excluding the GST collected, because GST is not your income. If in doubt, ask a CA.

Due date

For non-audit business or professional income (including presumptive ITR-4 filers), the due date is now 31 August after the year ends. The Finance Act 2026 changed this in both Acts. So ITR-4 for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is due 31 August 2026, and the return for tax year 2026-27 is due 31 August 2027 under Section 263(1). Salaried and other cases stay on 31 July.

Common mistakes

  • Using the scheme for a profession that is not specified.
  • Taking too much cash and losing the ₹75 lakh limit.
  • Including GST in gross receipts and paying income tax on it.
  • Paying advance tax in December instalments when one 15 March payment is allowed, or forgetting it entirely.
  • Filing ITR-4 while holding unlisted shares or foreign assets.
  • Not reconciling TDS in 26AS/AIS before filing.

Keep clean invoice records with the invoice maker. Your gross receipts total is then just a sum.

FAQ

What is the new section for 44ADA under the Income-tax Act 2025? The presumptive scheme for professionals is in Section 58(2), Table Sl. No. 3 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, according to the Income Tax Department's presumptive taxation FAQ.

What is the limit for presumptive taxation for professionals? Gross receipts up to ₹50 lakh, or up to ₹75 lakh if cash receipts do not exceed 5% of gross receipts. Income is deemed to be 50% of gross receipts, or higher if actually earned.

Who can file ITR-4 Sugam? Resident individuals, HUFs and firms (not LLPs) with presumptive business or professional income and total income up to ₹50 lakh, subject to exclusions such as being a company director, holding unlisted shares or having foreign assets (Rule 164, Income-tax Rules 2026).

Do presumptive taxpayers pay advance tax quarterly? No. Under Section 408(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, presumptive taxpayers pay their entire advance tax in one instalment by 15 March.

Can freelancers like designers or developers use the presumptive scheme for professionals? Only if their work counts as a specified profession under the Act. Check the current list in Section 62(4) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 or ask a CA.
