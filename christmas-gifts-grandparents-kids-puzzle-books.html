How to choose a gift they’ll actually use

  • Match print size to eyesight, not age. Many people in their 80s happily do normal puzzles, and some in their 50s struggle. If they hold the newspaper at arm’s length or say puzzles feel “cramped”, go extra-large.
  • Avoid the “too hard” trap. A puzzle book that feels like a test sits in a drawer. Easy-to-start books with answers at the back get finished, and a finished book feels good.
  • Add the small extras. A good soft pencil and eraser, a smooth gel pen or a reading lamp makes a puzzle book feel like a complete gift.
  • Make it shared. For kids, write a note like “Let’s do page 1 together on Christmas Day”. For grandparents, buy two copies of the same book so you can race each other on a video call.
  • For kids 3 to 8, pick one skill. Cutting practice builds hand strength for preschoolers, mazes build pencil control and planning, dot-to-dot builds counting, and color-by-number builds number recognition. One focused book beats a jumbled one for little ones.
  • Check delivery dates early. Print-on-demand books are printed when you order. Look at the date Amazon shows for your address, and leave a buffer in December.
  • Want a gift that is time together? Make a free advent activity calendar with one small family activity a day until Christmas.

Gift questions

What is a good Christmas gift for a grandparent who has everything?

Something to do rather than something to keep: a large print puzzle book with a good pen, a planned visit or call, or a game you can play together. Pick the print size for their eyesight, not their age.

Large print or extra-large print: which should I choose?

If they need reading glasses for normal books or say word searches feel cramped, choose extra-large print (bigger letters, smaller 12 x 12 grid). If they do puzzles already and see well with glasses, standard large print with a 15 x 15 grid gives more challenge.
