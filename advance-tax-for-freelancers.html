Advance Tax for Freelancers: 15 Dec Instalment Guide

Salaried people have tax deducted every month. Freelancers do not, so the law asks you to pay advance tax in instalments during the year. The next deadline is 15 December 2026.

Tax ki tension kam: thoda thoda bharo, March mein jhatka nahi lagega.

The 2026-27 schedule (new Act, same dates)

Under Section 408 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from tax year 2026-27, advance tax is paid cumulatively:

Due date Cumulative minimum paid
15 June 2026 15%
15 September 2026 45%
15 December 2026 75%
15 March 2027 100%

The Income Tax Department's transition FAQ confirms the dates and percentages are unchanged from the old Act. Only "assessment year" became "tax year".

Do I need to pay advance tax?

Advance tax applies if your estimated tax for the year, after TDS, is ₹10,000 or more (Section 404, Income-tax Act 2025, unchanged from the old Act). Resident senior citizens with no business or professional income are exempt. That exemption does not help a freelancer.

Many freelancers have TDS deducted by clients (10% on professional fees, see our TDS on freelance payments guide). TDS counts towards your liability, so you may owe less than you think, or nothing.

Presumptive (old 44ADA) freelancers: one instalment

If you use the presumptive scheme for professionals (old Section 44ADA, now Section 58 of the 2025 Act), the Department's FAQ says you pay all your advance tax in one instalment by 15 March, under Section 408(2). For you, 15 December is not a deadline, but saving towards March is still a good idea.

How to work out your 15 December figure

  1. Estimate total income for FY 2026-27: receipts so far + expected receipts to March − business expenses.
  2. Work out tax under the regime you will use (most people default to the new regime). [Slab rates not reproduced here. Check the incometax.gov.in tax calculator.]
  3. Subtract TDS already deducted or expected. Check Form 26AS/AIS.
  4. 75% of the result, minus what you paid in June and September = pay by 15 Dec.

Example (illustrative): estimated tax ₹80,000, expected TDS ₹30,000, so advance tax = ₹50,000. - 75% = ₹37,500 - Paid so far: ₹15,000 - Pay by 15 Dec: ₹22,500

Our free TDS calculator helps you add up TDS, and the GST calculator helps you separate GST you have collected (not income) from your actual fee.

What if I miss it?

Under the 2025 Act: s.424 (was 234B), 1% a month if advance tax paid is under 90% of assessed tax. s.425 (was 234C), 3% on shortfalls at 15 Jun/15 Sep/15 Dec and 1% at 15 Mar. Rates unchanged. Paying late is still better than not paying.

How to pay

On incometax.gov.in use e-Pay Tax and choose advance tax for the correct tax year (2026-27). Keep the challan details. You will need them when you file your return.

A simple habit for irregular income

Har payment pe thoda side mein rakho. 15 Dec ko bas click karo, tax ki tension kam.

Common mistakes freelancers make

  • Counting GST receipts as income. GST you collect belongs to the government, not to you.
  • Forgetting foreign-client income. Export receipts are usually zero-rated for GST, but they are still taxable income.
  • Choosing the wrong tax year on the challan. Pick 2026-27 for income earned between April 2026 and March 2027.
  • Ignoring TDS mismatches. If a client's TDS is missing from 26AS, follow up now, not in July.

Worked example: what TDS does to your instalment

Two freelancers each expect ₹1,00,000 of tax for the year (illustrative):

Neha (clients deduct TDS) Kabir (foreign clients, no TDS)
Estimated tax ₹1,00,000 ₹1,00,000
Expected TDS ₹60,000 ₹0
Advance tax due ₹40,000 ₹1,00,000
75% by 15 Dec ₹30,000 ₹75,000
Paid Jun + Sep ₹18,000 ₹45,000
Pay by 15 Dec ₹12,000 ₹30,000

Kabir's December cheque is much larger even though his tax is the same. Freelancers paid by foreign clients usually feel advance tax the most. Add up your expected TDS with the TDS calculator.

Re-estimate every quarter

Your estimate in June is a guess. By December you have eight months of actual figures, so redo the calculation. If income dropped, you may need to pay less. If a big project landed, pay more now rather than all of it in March.

Keep your challan records tidy

After each payment, save the challan receipt in a "Tax 2026-27" folder alongside your TDS certificates. When you file your return, you will need the challan details for every instalment. Having them in one place turns a stressful afternoon into a ten-minute job.

Worked example: catching up after a missed September

Say your advance tax for the year is ₹60,000 and you paid nothing in June or September. By 15 December you need 75% in total, which is ₹45,000. Paying the full ₹45,000 now stops the shortfall growing. Interest for the missed June and September instalments still applies under s.425, but it is smaller than letting it run until March.

FAQ

When is the third advance tax instalment due for 2026-27? 15 December 2026. By then at least 75% of the estimated advance tax should be paid in total, under Section 408 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Do freelancers have to pay advance tax? Yes, if their estimated tax for the year after TDS reaches the advance tax threshold. TDS deducted by clients counts towards it.

Does a freelancer under presumptive taxation pay advance tax in December? No. Under the 2025 Act, taxpayers using the presumptive scheme (Section 58, formerly 44ADA) pay their entire advance tax by 15 March, according to the Income Tax Department FAQ.

Did the advance tax dates change under the new Income-tax Act 2025? No. The Income Tax Department says the instalment dates and percentages (15/45/75/100%) are unchanged. Only the reference changed from assessment year to tax year.

How do I pay advance tax online? Use the e-Pay Tax service on incometax.gov.in, choose advance tax and the correct tax year, and keep the challan details for your return.

Who has to pay advance tax? Anyone whose estimated tax for the year, after TDS, is ₹10,000 or more (Section 404, Income-tax Act 2025, unchanged from the old Act). Resident individuals aged 60 or more with no business or professional income are exempt (Section 403(3)).

What interest applies if I miss an advance tax instalment? You pay 1% a month interest (old 234B/234C): on the shortfall if advance tax paid is under 90% of assessed tax, and on shortfalls at each instalment date. The rates are unchanged under the Income-tax Act 2025.
