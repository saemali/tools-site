# LUT for Export of Services: How Freelancers File RFD-11 (FY 2026-27)

Working for clients in the US, UK, UAE or Australia? If you are GST-registered, a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** lets you invoice those clients **without charging or paying IGST**. Without one, you either pay 18% IGST and wait for a refund, or you get it wrong. Here is how to do it properly.

*Tax ki tension kam: foreign client, zero GST, bas ek LUT pehle file karo.*

## Step 1: is your work really an "export of services"?

Under Section 2(6) of the IGST Act, a supply counts as an export of services only if **all five** of these are true:
1. You (the supplier) are **located in India**.
2. The client (the recipient) is **located outside India**.
3. The **place of supply** is outside India.
4. You are paid in **convertible foreign exchange**, or in Indian rupees where the RBI permits.
5. You and the client are **not just two establishments of the same person** (for example, your own branch abroad).

Typical freelancer cases, such as a Bengaluru developer billing a Berlin startup and paid in EUR or USD to an Indian bank account, usually meet all five. For most freelance services (software, design, consulting delivered online), Section 13(2) of the IGST Act makes the place of supply the recipient's location, so it is outside India. Exceptions in Sections 13(3) to 13(13) include services on goods physically present in India, immovable property, events, and intermediary services.

## Step 2: understand what the LUT does

An export of services is a **zero-rated supply**. You have two options:
- **Export under LUT:** no IGST on the invoice, nothing to pay, nothing to claim back.
- **Pay IGST and claim a refund:** you pay 18% up front and wait for the refund.

For most freelancers the LUT is simpler. The GST portal guide says the LUT has to be furnished **before** the export supply, in **Form GST RFD-11**, by a registered person who has not been prosecuted for tax evasion of ₹2.5 crore or more.

## Step 3: file RFD-11 on the portal (about 10 minutes)

From the GST portal user guide:
1. Log in at gst.gov.in.
2. Go to **Services > User Services > Furnish Letter of Undertaking (LUT)**.
3. Choose the **financial year** (2026-27).
4. Enter the name, address and occupation of **two independent witnesses**.
5. Tick the self-declarations.
6. Sign with **DSC or EVC** and submit. Download the ARN/acknowledgement.

**Validity:** an LUT is valid for the **whole financial year** in which it is filed (CBIC master circular). The portal marks it **Expired** at the end of that FY. File a fresh one each year, ideally in early April before the first export invoice. If you get a **new GSTIN**, file a fresh LUT for the rest of the year.

## Step 4: invoice correctly

Your export invoice should show:
- Your GSTIN, invoice number and date
- Client name and overseas address
- Description of the service and SAC
- Value (in foreign currency and/or INR)
- **IGST: 0** or "Nil"
- The endorsement required by CGST Rule 46: **"SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT/SUPPLY TO SEZ UNIT OR SEZ DEVELOPER FOR AUTHORISED OPERATIONS UNDER BOND OR LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX"**. The invoice must also show the recipient\'s name and address, the delivery address and the country of destination. Adding your LUT ARN is good practice.

You can draft it in our free [invoice maker](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/invoice.html).

## Step 5: get paid in time

Under **Rule 96A** of the CGST Rules, if payment for exported services is **not received** within **one year** of the invoice date (or the longer period allowed under FEMA/RBI, whichever is later), plus **15 days**, you have to pay the IGST with interest. If that happens and you do not pay, the LUT facility is **withdrawn** until you do (CBIC circular).

## Worked example: Neha's first export year

Neha, a registered UX designer in Mumbai, files her LUT on 3 April 2026. In FY 2026-27 she bills:
- A US client: USD 2,000 a month, about ₹1,70,000 at an illustrative rate
- An Indian client: ₹50,000 a month

Her GST each month:
- US invoice: **IGST 0** (export under LUT)
- Indian invoice (same state): CGST ₹4,500 + SGST ₹4,500

She pays **₹9,000 a month**, not ₹9,000 + ₹30,600. Without an LUT she would have had to pay IGST of about ₹30,600 a month on the US invoice (18% of ₹1,70,000) and then claim a refund. Check the numbers in our [GST calculator](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gst.html).

## Worked example: a late payment

Arjun invoices a UK client on 10 June 2026 under LUT. The client has still not paid by June 2027. Unless a longer FEMA/RBI period applies, once one year plus 15 days have passed Arjun owes IGST on that invoice with interest. Chasing overseas payments is a tax matter too, not just about cash flow.

## Do you even need GST registration?

Export turnover **counts** towards aggregate turnover for the registration threshold (₹20 lakh for most service providers under Section 22). If all your clients are abroad and your total turnover is under ₹20 lakh, you may not need to register at all, and then no LUT is needed either. See our [GST registration threshold guide](12-gst-registration-threshold.md).

## Common LUT mistakes

- **Filing the LUT after you have already issued export invoices.**
- **Forgetting to renew** in April, so the old LUT expires on 31 March.
- **Billing a foreign client's Indian subsidiary** and treating it as an export (the client is located in India).
- **Getting paid through routes that may not count as convertible foreign exchange,** without checking the RBI position.
- **Leaving export invoices out of GSTR-1.** Zero-rated supplies still have to be reported.
- **Assuming "export" means no income tax.** Export receipts are still taxable income, see our [advance tax guide](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/advance-tax-for-freelancers.html).

*LUT April mein, invoice pe zero IGST, aur payment time pe. Tax ki tension kam, dollars ki khushi zyada.*

## FAQ

**What is LUT in GST for export of services?**
A Letter of Undertaking in Form GST RFD-11 lets a registered person export goods or services without paying IGST. It must be filed on the GST portal before the export supply.

**How long is a GST LUT valid?**
An LUT is valid for the financial year in which it is filed. The portal marks it expired at the end of that year, so a fresh LUT is needed each financial year.

**Do freelancers need an LUT for foreign clients?**
GST-registered freelancers who want to bill foreign clients without paying IGST should file an LUT before issuing export invoices. Unregistered freelancers below the threshold do not charge GST.

**What happens if a foreign client doesn't pay on time under LUT?**
Under CGST Rule 96A, if payment is not received within one year (or the period allowed under FEMA/RBI, whichever is later) plus 15 days from the invoice date, the exporter must pay the IGST with interest.

**What makes a service an export under GST?**
Under IGST Act Section 2(6): the supplier is in India, the recipient is outside India, the place of supply is outside India, payment is in convertible foreign exchange (or INR where the RBI permits), and the parties are not establishments of the same person.
