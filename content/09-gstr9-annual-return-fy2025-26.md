# GSTR-9 Annual Return for FY 2025-26: Who Must File, Due Date & Checklist

Every December, small business owners ask the same question: "Do I really have to file GSTR-9?" For many small taxpayers the answer is now **no**. Here is who has to file, when, and how to avoid the mistakes that cost money.

*Tax ki tension kam: saal bhar ka hisaab ek baar, sahi tarike se.*

## What GSTR-9 is

GSTR-9 is the **annual return** under Section 44 of the CGST Act, read with Rule 80 of the CGST Rules. It brings together a whole financial year: your sales (from GSTR-1), the tax you paid and the ITC you took (from GSTR-3B), plus some extra details. It is not a new tax. It is a year-end summary of what you already reported every month or quarter.

## Who has to file for FY 2025-26?

| Your aggregate turnover in FY 2025-26 | GSTR-9 | GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2 crore | **Optional / exempt** (Notification 15/2025-CT) | Not required |
| Above ₹2 crore, up to ₹5 crore | **Required** | Not required |
| Above ₹5 crore | **Required** | **Required** (self-certified, Rule 80(3)) |

Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (17 Sep 2025) exempts registered persons whose aggregate turnover in a financial year is up to ₹2 crore from filing the annual return, from FY 2024-25 onwards. Most freelancers and small shops fall in this group.

Composition taxpayers, Input Service Distributors, casual and non-resident taxable persons and TDS/TCS deductors are outside the normal GSTR-9 rules (Rule 80 lists them separately).

## The due date

The GST portal's GSTR-9 FAQ says the annual return is due by **31 December of the following financial year**, unless the Government extends it by notification. For FY 2025-26 that is **31 December 2026**. A late fee applies if you file after the due date. The portal works it out automatically and you cannot file without paying it.

## Why the date matters for ITC too

Under Section 16(4) of the CGST Act, you lose the right to take FY 2025-26 ITC on the **earlier** of 30 November 2026 or the date you file GSTR-9. If you file GSTR-9 in October, your ITC window closes in October. Finish your ITC clean-up first. Our [ITC last date guide](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/itc-claim-last-date-2026.html) walks through it.

## Worked example: should Priya file?

Priya runs a design studio in Pune. FY 2025-26:
- Taxable services to Indian clients: ₹1,40,00,000
- Export of services under LUT: ₹40,00,000
- **Aggregate turnover: ₹1.8 crore** (exports count towards aggregate turnover)

₹1.8 crore is below ₹2 crore, so under Notification 15/2025 GSTR-9 is **optional** for her. If her exports had been ₹70 lakh, aggregate turnover would be ₹2.1 crore and she would **have to file** by 31 Dec 2026.

## Worked example: catching a mismatch

Ravi's FY 2025-26 figures:
- GSTR-1 taxable value total: ₹3,12,00,000
- GSTR-3B taxable value total: ₹3,10,50,000
- Difference: **₹1,50,000**

He finds a March 2026 invoice that was reported in GSTR-1 but left out of the March GSTR-3B. At 18% that is ₹27,000 of tax not yet paid. GSTR-9 is where he declares it, and any unpaid tax has to be paid in cash with interest under Section 50, using Form GST DRC-03 (voluntary payment under Section 73(5)/74(5); from FY 2024-25 Section 74A). Spotting it now is far cheaper than spotting it in a notice.

## A practical GSTR-9 checklist

1. **Confirm you have to file.** Add up your aggregate turnover for FY 2025-26 (taxable + exempt + exports + inter-state supplies, for your PAN across India).
2. **Download all 12 GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filings** (or the quarterly ones if you are on QRMP).
3. **Match GSTR-1 against GSTR-3B** month by month and list the differences.
4. **Match ITC in GSTR-3B against GSTR-2B.** Note anything claimed that is not in 2B.
5. **Match the GST figures against your books.** Sales, purchases and credit notes.
6. **Leave GSTR-9 until your ITC is final,** because filing it ends the ITC window.
7. **Pay any shortfall** before or while filing.
8. **Preview, then file** with DSC or EVC, and download the filed copy.

## Common GSTR-9 mistakes

- **Filing when you did not need to,** and closing your ITC window early.
- **Leaving out exports or exempt sales** when checking whether you cross ₹2 crore.
- **Treating GSTR-9 as a fresh return** and entering figures that do not match the returns you filed.
- **Trying to claim new ITC in GSTR-9.** The annual return cannot open up credit you lost after the Section 16(4) date.
- **Ignoring HSN summaries** for outward supplies, which the form asks for.
- **Waiting until 30–31 December,** when the portal is busiest.

## Exempt, but thinking of filing anyway?

Being exempt does not stop you filing. Some businesses file anyway to keep a tidy annual record. If you do, remember the Section 16(4) point above: filing closes your FY 2025-26 ITC window that day. So if you file voluntarily, file after your last ITC claim, never before.

## If you are a small freelancer

Turnover under ₹2 crore? You can usually skip GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26. You still have to file every GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B on time, see our [GSTR-1 due date guide](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gstr-1-due-date-november-2026.html) and [GSTR-3B due date guide](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gstr-3b-due-date-october-2026.html).

*Turnover 2 crore se kam? GSTR-9 ka tension nahi. Zyada hai? 31 December se pehle aaram se niptao, tax ki tension kam.*

## FAQ

**What is the due date of GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26?**
31 December 2026, per the GST portal's rule of 31 December following the financial year, unless the Government extends it by notification.

**Is GSTR-9 mandatory for turnover below ₹2 crore?**
No. Notification 15/2025-Central Tax exempts registered persons with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore in a financial year from filing the annual return, from FY 2024-25 onwards.

**Who has to file GSTR-9C?**
Registered persons whose aggregate turnover in the financial year exceeds ₹5 crore must file a self-certified reconciliation statement in GSTR-9C along with GSTR-9 (CGST Rule 80(3)).

**Is there a late fee for GSTR-9?**
Yes. The GST portal calculates a late fee if GSTR-9 is filed after the due date, and the return cannot be filed without paying it.

**Does filing GSTR-9 affect my ITC?**
Yes. Under Section 16(4), ITC for a year cannot be taken after the earlier of 30 November of the next year or the date you file GSTR-9. Filing early closes the window early.
