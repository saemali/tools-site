# GST Registration Threshold 2026: ₹20 Lakh, ₹40 Lakh or ₹10 Lakh?

"Do I need a GST number?" is probably the most common tax question new freelancers and small sellers ask. The answer depends on **three things**: what you sell (goods, services or both), **where** your business is, and whether any **compulsory registration** rule applies to you.

*Tax ki tension kam: limit samjho, phir decide karo GSTIN chahiye ya nahi.*

## The quick table

| You supply | Most states | Special category states* |
|---|---|---|
| **Services** (or goods + services) | **₹20 lakh** | **₹10 lakh** |
| **Goods only** | **₹40 lakh** in states that adopted it, otherwise ₹20 lakh | ₹10 lakh (or as notified, up to ₹20 lakh) |

*Special category states for this purpose: Section 22 of the CGST Act uses the Constitution's list but **excludes** Jammu & Kashmir, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Himachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Sikkim and Uttarakhand. That leaves **Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura** on the ₹10 lakh limit. For goods, Notification 10/2019-CT lets states opt out of the ₹40L limit; see the state table.

You must register once your aggregate turnover in a financial year **exceeds** the limit, so being exactly at ₹20 lakh does not trigger it.

## Where these numbers come from

- **₹20 lakh / ₹10 lakh:** Section 22(1) of the CGST Act and its first proviso.
- **₹40 lakh for goods-only suppliers:** Notification 10/2019-Central Tax (from 1 April 2019) exempts anyone who **exclusively supplies goods** with aggregate turnover up to ₹40 lakh, **except**:
  - people who must register compulsorily under Section 24
  - suppliers of ice cream, pan masala and tobacco products
  - intra-state suppliers in **Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Puducherry, Sikkim, Telangana, Tripura and Uttarakhand**
  - people who choose to register voluntarily or want to stay registered
- The PIB release on the 2019 decision confirms the threshold for **service providers stayed at ₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in special category states).

## What counts in "aggregate turnover"

Aggregate turnover is calculated **for your PAN across India** and includes taxable supplies, **exempt supplies, exports** and inter-state supplies. It **excludes** CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and compensation cess, and the value of inward supplies on which you pay tax under reverse charge (CGST Act s.2(6)).

## Worked example: a freelancer with foreign clients

Rahul, a developer in Hyderabad, FY 2026-27:
- Indian clients: ₹8,00,000
- US clients (export): ₹14,00,000
- **Aggregate turnover: ₹22,00,000**

He crosses ₹20 lakh, so he **must register**, even though most of his income is exports. Once registered, he can invoice his US clients without IGST under an LUT (see our [LUT guide](11-lut-export-of-services.md)).

## Worked example: a goods seller in Maharashtra vs Telangana

Two people each sell handmade candles worth ₹30 lakh a year, only within their own state:
- **Maharashtra:** goods only, under ₹40 lakh, not in the excluded list, so **no registration needed** (unless Section 24 applies).
- **Telangana:** Telangana is in Notification 10/2019's excluded list for intra-state supplies, so the ₹20 lakh limit applies and the seller **must register**.

Same business, different answer, because of the state (as per Notification 10/2019-CT and later state notifications; check your state's current position).

## Worked example: a mixed supplier

Sana sells printed T-shirts (goods) for ₹18 lakh and also takes design commissions (services) for ₹5 lakh. Her aggregate turnover is ₹23 lakh. Because she is **not exclusively** a goods supplier, the ₹40 lakh exemption does not apply. She is over ₹20 lakh, so she **must register**.

## Compulsory registration (whatever your turnover)

Section 24 lists people who have to register **regardless of turnover**. Examples include people making inter-state taxable supplies (of goods), people liable under reverse charge, casual and non-resident taxable persons, and many sellers who supply through **e-commerce operators**. Some of these have been relaxed by later notifications. Notification 10/2017-IT exempts people making inter-state supplies of **services** below the threshold. Notification 65/2017-CT exempts e-commerce **service** suppliers below the threshold, except s.9(5) services. From 1 Oct 2023, Notification 34/2023-CT exempts small **goods** sellers on e-commerce who meet its conditions (for example, no inter-state supplies of goods, selling through e-commerce in only one state, and enrolment on the GST portal).

If you sell goods on a marketplace, check this carefully before you list.

## Voluntary registration: worth it?

You can register voluntarily below the limit. Things to weigh:
- **Pros:** you can claim ITC on business purchases, B2B clients often prefer GST-registered vendors, and you can export under LUT.
- **Cons:** monthly or quarterly returns (see our [GSTR-3B](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gstr-3b-due-date-october-2026.html) and [GSTR-1](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gstr-1-due-date-november-2026.html) guides), and you have to charge GST, which makes you 18% dearer to clients who cannot claim it back (see the [GST rate guide](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gst-rate-for-freelancers-2026.html)).

Check what 18% does to your quote in the [GST calculator](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/gst.html).

## The month you cross the limit

Under Section 25(1), apply within **30 days** of becoming liable (casual and non-resident taxable persons apply at least 5 days before starting business). Apply on gst.gov.in under Services > Registration > New Registration, with PAN, Aadhaar, proof of business address and bank details. Until the GSTIN arrives, do not show GST on invoices. Keep records so you can issue revised invoices if needed.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting only domestic income** and leaving out exports or exempt sales.
- **Assuming ₹40 lakh applies to services.** It applies only to goods-only suppliers, and not in every state.
- **Charging GST without a GSTIN.** That is not allowed. Issue a plain invoice from the [invoice maker](https://saemali.github.io/tools-site/invoice.html) instead.
- **Waiting until year-end** to check turnover. Track it monthly.
- **Ignoring Section 24** when selling online or across states.
- **Thinking a GSTIN is per person.** Registration is per state where you make taxable supplies.

## FAQ

**What is the GST registration limit for freelancers in 2026?**
For service providers it is ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover in a financial year, or ₹10 lakh in special category states, under Section 22 of the CGST Act.

**Is the GST registration limit ₹40 lakh?**
Only for people who exclusively supply goods, in states that have adopted it, under Notification 10/2019-Central Tax. It excludes certain states, ice cream, pan masala and tobacco, and compulsory registrations.

**Does export income count towards the GST registration limit?**
Yes. Aggregate turnover includes exports and exempt supplies, so foreign-client income counts towards the ₹20 lakh threshold.

**Do I need GST registration if I sell on Amazon or Flipkart?**
Possibly, whatever your turnover. Section 24 makes registration compulsory for many e-commerce sellers, subject to later exemptions. Check the current rules before listing.

**Can I register for GST voluntarily below the threshold?**
Yes. Voluntary registration lets you claim ITC and work with B2B clients, but you must then charge GST and file regular returns.
