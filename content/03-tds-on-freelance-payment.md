# TDS on Freelance Payments: 194J vs 194C, Calculated Simply

A client paid you ₹45,000 for a ₹50,000 invoice and said "TDS kaat liya". Was that right? Will you get the ₹5,000 back? And why does your CA keep saying "Section 393" when everyone online says 194J?

*Tax ki tension kam: TDS kat gaya matlab paisa gaya nahi. Woh aapke naam pe jama hai.*

## First, the 2026 name change

From **1 April 2026**, the **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act. The Income Tax Department's FAQ explains that the old TDS sections 192 to 194T have been consolidated into **Section 392** (salary) and **Section 393** (everything else). The Department also says the **rates and thresholds were kept the same**. Only the section numbers changed.

| What people search | Old Act (1961) | New Act (2025), from 1 Apr 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor / "work" payments | Section 194C | Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(i) |
| Professional / technical fees | Section 194J | Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(iii) |

Which Act applies depends on the **earlier of credit or payment**. If that happened on or before 31 March 2026, the old Act applies. If it happened on or after 1 April 2026, the new Act applies.

## The rates, in plain numbers

**Professional / technical fees (old 194J, new 393(1) Sl. 6(iii))**
- **10%** on fees for professional services
- **2%** on fees for technical services (not professional), and a few other specified cases
- Threshold: **₹50,000** a year from one payer (for most categories)

**Contractor payments (old 194C, new 393(1) Sl. 6(i))**
- **1%** if you are an individual or HUF
- **2%** for others (firms, companies)
- Threshold: more than **₹30,000** in a single payment, or more than **₹1,00,000** in total during the year

*Note: one Income Tax Department Budget-2026 FAQ PDF lists the contractor rates the other way round (2% individual / 1% others). The Department's TDS Compliance page and the Act say the rates are unchanged from 194C (1% / 2%), so we use those. **[Flag for review: official sources contradict each other here.]***

No PAN? TDS can go up to **20%** under the higher-rate rule. Give your PAN to every client.

## Which one applies to a freelancer?

That depends on what you sell, not on what you call yourself.

- **Professional services**, meaning work that relies on specialised skill or judgement (for example legal, medical, engineering, architecture, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration and other notified professions): usually **10%**.
- **Technical services**, such as managerial, technical or consultancy services that are not professional: **2%**.
- **"Work" under a contract**, which includes advertising, production on order and certain content production: the **contractor rate, 1% or 2%**.

In practice, many clients deduct 10% from designers, developers and writers to be safe. If you think the lower rate applies, raise it politely with documentation. Do not just argue.

## Calculated simply

Invoice: ₹60,000 professional fee + 18% GST = ₹70,800.

If GST is shown separately on the invoice or contract, TDS is calculated on the amount **excluding GST** (CBDT Circular 23/2017). This circular continues under the Income-tax Act 2025, because s.536(2)(j) keeps old circulars valid where consistent. If GST is not shown separately, TDS applies to the gross amount.

- TDS @ 10% on ₹60,000 = **₹6,000**
- You receive ₹70,800 − ₹6,000 = **₹64,800**

## Getting your TDS back

TDS is **advance tax paid in your name**, not a fee.
1. Check that it shows in your **Form 26AS / AIS** on incometax.gov.in.
2. Ask the client for the **TDS certificate**.
3. When you file your return, the TDS is set off against your tax. If your total tax is lower than the TDS, you get a **refund**.

Many freelancers with modest income get most of their TDS back.

## Worked example: a year with three clients

Arjun, a consultant, invoices three clients in FY 2026-27 (amounts before GST):

| Client | Fees for the year | Above ₹50,000? | TDS @ 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | ₹3,00,000 | Yes | ₹30,000 |
| B | ₹1,20,000 | Yes | ₹12,000 |
| C | ₹40,000 | No | ₹0 |

Total TDS: **₹42,000**. If Arjun's final tax for the year comes to ₹25,000, he can claim a **₹17,000 refund** when he files his return, provided all of it shows in 26AS. If client B's ₹12,000 is missing from 26AS, he cannot claim it until B corrects their TDS return. That is why the tracking sheet matters.

## Common TDS mistakes

- **Treating TDS as lost income** and quoting too low to make up for it.
- **Not reconciling 26AS** before filing your return.
- **Forgetting to account for TDS when you plan advance tax.** It counts towards your liability.
- **Not giving clients your PAN,** which pushes the rate up to 20%.

## FAQ

**What is the TDS rate on freelance payments?**
Fees for professional services generally attract 10% TDS and technical services 2%, once payments from one payer exceed ₹50,000 in a year. Contract work is 1% for individuals/HUF and 2% for others. From 1 April 2026 these sit under Section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

**Is 194J still valid after 1 April 2026?**
For payments or credits from 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies, and professional fees fall under Section 393(1) Table Sl. No. 6(iii). The Income Tax Department says the rates and thresholds are unchanged.

**What is the difference between 194J and 194C?**
194J (now 393(1) Sl. 6(iii)) covers professional and technical fees at 10%/2%. 194C (now 393(1) Sl. 6(i)) covers payments for work under a contract at 1%/2%.

**Is TDS deducted on the GST amount?**
Where GST is shown separately on the invoice, TDS is generally deducted on the amount excluding GST, based on an earlier CBDT circular. Check how this applies under the 2025 Act.

**How do freelancers get TDS refunded?**
Check that the TDS appears in Form 26AS/AIS, then claim it when you file your income tax return. Any excess over your final tax liability is refunded.
