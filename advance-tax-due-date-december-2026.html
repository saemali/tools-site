Advance Tax Due Date 15 December 2026 (3rd Instalment) + Calculator

The third advance tax instalment for FY 2026-27 is due on Tuesday, 15 December 2026. By that day, at least 75% of your tax for the year (after TDS/TCS) should be paid in total. This page shows the full schedule, who has to pay, what happens if you pay less, how to pay on the portal, and a small calculator for your December figure.

15 December se pehle 75% jama kar dijiye, interest se bach jaayenge.

The quick answer

Instalment (FY 2026-27)Due dateCumulative minimum paid
1stMonday, 15 June 2026 (passed)15%
2ndTuesday, 15 September 2026 (passed)45%
3rdTuesday, 15 December 202675%
4thMonday, 15 March 2027100%
Presumptive (Section 58 / old 44AD, 44ADA)Monday, 15 March 2027100% in one instalment

The percentages are running totals, not separate slices. If your advance tax for the year is ₹1,00,000, you need ₹75,000 paid in total by 15 December, including what you paid in June and September. Source: Section 408 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies to income earned from 1 April 2026 (tax year 2026-27).

How the calculator works: advance tax = estimated tax − TDS/TCS. If that is under ₹10,000, no advance tax is needed. Otherwise 75% of it, minus what you have already paid, is due by 15 December. The interest figure is 3% of any December shortfall under Section 425(1) (old 234C). It does not include interest for a missed June or September instalment, Section 424 (old 234B) interest, or any rounding the Department applies. It is an estimate, not a tax computation.

Who has to pay advance tax?

  • Tax of ₹10,000 or more after TDS/TCS. Under Section 404 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, you pay advance tax if your tax for the year, worked out under the advance tax provisions, is ₹10,000 or more. The Income Tax Department says this threshold is unchanged from the old Act.
  • Senior citizens without business income are exempt. Section 403(3): a resident individual who is 60 or older at any time in the year and has no income under "Profits and gains of business or profession" does not pay advance tax. A retired person with only pension and interest is covered. A senior citizen who freelances or runs a business is not.
  • Salaried people usually have enough TDS from salary. If you also have interest, rent, capital gains or side-income with little TDS, check whether the remaining tax crosses ₹10,000.
  • Freelancers and professionals are the group most often caught out, especially with foreign clients who deduct no TDS. See our advance tax guide for freelancers.

Presumptive taxpayers (44AD / 44ADA): no December instalment

If you declare income under the presumptive scheme, now Section 58 of the 2025 Act (Table Sl. No. 1 is the old 44AD business scheme, Sl. No. 3 the old 44ADA professional scheme), Section 408(2) says you pay the whole advance tax by 15 March 2027. Nothing is due on 15 December. If you pay less than the full amount by 15 March, Section 425(3) charges 1% on the shortfall. Section 408(2) names only Sl. No. 1 and 3, so the goods-carriage scheme (old 44AE, Sl. No. 2) is not covered by the single-instalment rule. More: ITR-4 for presumptive professionals.

What if I pay less than 75% by 15 December?

  • Section 425 (old 234C), deferment interest: 3% on the shortfall at the 15 June, 15 September and 15 December instalments, and 1% at 15 March. Under the old Act this was described as 1% a month for three months. The shortfall is measured against 15%/45%/75%/100% of the tax due on your returned income, after TDS/TCS.
  • The 12% / 36% rule: under Section 425(2), no interest is charged for the June instalment if you had paid at least 12% by 15 June, or for September if you had paid at least 36% by 15 September. There is no such margin for December: 75% is the line.
  • Section 424 (old 234B), default interest: if the advance tax you pay by 31 March 2027 is less than 90% of the assessed tax, you pay 1% a month (or part of a month) from 1 April 2027 on the shortfall until the tax is paid or assessed.
  • Income you could not foresee: Section 425(4) waives interest on a shortfall caused by capital gains, dividends, or business income arising for the first time, if you pay the tax on it in the remaining instalments or by 31 March.

Example: advance tax ₹2,00,000, paid ₹90,000 so far. 75% is ₹1,50,000, so ₹60,000 is due by 15 December. If you pay nothing more, interest for the December instalment is about 3% × ₹60,000 = ₹1,800. Paying late is still better than not paying, because the 234B-type interest keeps running after March.

How to pay advance tax online

  1. Go to incometax.gov.in. Log in and open e-File → e-Pay Tax, or use e-Pay Tax under Quick Links without logging in (PAN + mobile OTP).
  2. Click New Payment and choose the Income Tax tile.
  3. Select the Income-tax Act, 2025, tax year 2026-27, and type of payment (minor head) Advance Tax (100). For 2025-Act payments the portal calls the challan ITNS 280N; the older ITNS 280 is used for years under the 1961 Act.
  4. Enter the amount, choose net banking, debit card, UPI/card through the payment gateway, RTGS/NEFT or pay at a bank counter, and pay.
  5. Download the challan receipt from Payment History. Note the BSR code, date and challan serial number for your return.

Picking the wrong Act or year is the most common mistake. The Department's FAQ says advance tax on income from April 2026 to March 2027 must be paid against tax year 2026-27, so the credit lands in the right year.

Checklist before 15 December

  1. Re-estimate income. You now have eight months of actual numbers. Add expected income to March.
  2. Work out the tax under the regime you will use. Our old vs new regime calculator helps you compare.
  3. Subtract TDS/TCS. Check Form 26AS / AIS. For freelance fees, see our TDS calculator.
  4. Take 75%, subtract what you've paid, and pay the rest. Don't leave it to the evening of the 15th.
  5. Still filing last year's return? The belated/revised return for FY 2025-26 has its own deadline. See belated ITR last date 2026.

FAQ

What is the advance tax due date in December 2026?
Tuesday, 15 December 2026. It is the third instalment for tax year 2026-27 (FY 2026-27). By then you should have paid at least 75% of your advance tax for the year in total, under Section 408(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

How much advance tax should I have paid by 15 December 2026?
At least 75% of your estimated tax for the year after TDS/TCS, as a running total. Subtract what you paid on or before 15 June and 15 September; the rest is your December payment.

Do 44AD or 44ADA presumptive taxpayers pay advance tax on 15 December?
No. Under Section 408(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, people declaring income under the presumptive scheme in Section 58 (Table Sl. No. 1 or 3, the old 44AD and 44ADA) pay their whole advance tax in one instalment by 15 March 2027. The Income Tax Department says this is unchanged from the old Act.

Who has to pay advance tax for FY 2026-27?
Anyone whose tax for the year, after TDS/TCS, is ₹10,000 or more (Section 404, Income-tax Act 2025). A resident individual aged 60 or more at any time in the year who has no business or professional income does not have to pay advance tax (Section 403(3)).

What interest applies if I pay less than 75% by 15 December?
Under Section 425 (old 234C), interest of 3% on the shortfall below 75% (the old Act described it as 1% a month for three months). Separately, if your total advance tax paid by 31 March is under 90% of the assessed tax, Section 424 (old 234B) charges 1% a month from 1 April 2027 until the tax is paid or assessed.

Does the 12%/36% relief apply to the December instalment?
No. Section 425(2) only waives interest for the June instalment if you paid at least 12% by 15 June, and for the September instalment if you paid at least 36% by 15 September. There is no such margin for 15 December.

Did the Income-tax Act 2025 change the advance tax dates or section numbers?
The dates and percentages did not change. The section numbers did: the instalment schedule is now Section 408, the ₹10,000 threshold Section 404, the senior-citizen exemption Section 403(3), and interest Sections 424 (old 234B) and 425 (old 234C). Presumptive schemes 44AD/44ADA are now Section 58.

Which challan do I use to pay advance tax for FY 2026-27?
On the e-filing portal, go to e-Pay Tax, select the Income-tax Act, 2025, tax year 2026-27 and minor head 100 (Advance Tax). For Income-tax Act 2025 payments the portal calls this challan ITNS 280N. The older ITNS 280 is for years under the 1961 Act.
