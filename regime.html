Rates and limits used

  • New regime (default, s.202 Income-tax Act 2025): nil up to ₹4L; 5% ₹4–8L; 10% ₹8–12L; 15% ₹12–16L; 20% ₹16–20L; 25% ₹20–24L; 30% above ₹24L. Standard deduction ₹75,000 on salary.
  • Rebate (s.156, old 87A): new regime, up to ₹60,000 if taxable income is ₹12L or less, with marginal relief just above ₹12L; old regime, up to ₹12,500 if taxable income is ₹5L or less.
  • Old regime: nil up to ₹2.5L (₹3L if 60–79, ₹5L if 80+); 5% to ₹5L; 20% ₹5–10L; 30% above ₹10L. Standard deduction ₹50,000.
  • Old-regime deductions: 80C ₹1.5L; 80CCD(1B) NPS ₹50,000; 80D ₹25,000 for self/family (₹50,000 if senior) plus ₹25,000 for parents (₹50,000 if senior); home-loan interest ₹2L.
  • HRA: least of HRA received; rent minus 10% of basic+DA; 50% of basic+DA in Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad and Bengaluru (four cities added from tax year 2026-27 by the Income-tax Rules 2026), 40% elsewhere.
  • Cess: 4% health and education cess. Surcharge (income above ₹50L) is not included.

The Finance Bill 2026 memorandum states there is no change in tax rates for tax year 2026-27. Deductions use their familiar old section numbers; the 2025 Act renumbers them (80C is now s.123).

Official sources: Finance Bill 2026 memorandum, Threshold limits under the Income-tax Act, incometax.gov.in: old vs new slabs, Income-tax Rules 2026.

Common questions · aksar pooche jaane wale sawaal

Is the new regime the default for FY 2026-27?

Yes. The new regime under section 202 of the Income-tax Act 2025 is the default; you must opt out to use the old regime.

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free in the new regime?

For a resident individual the rebate makes tax nil when taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh (₹12.75 lakh salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction), excluding special-rate income such as capital gains.

Which cities get 50% HRA?

From tax year 2026-27: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. Everywhere else is 40%.
