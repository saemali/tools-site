GSTR-3B Due Date October 2026: Monthly & QRMP (20/22/24 Oct) Explained

October is a busy month. Navratri, Dussehra, Diwali shopping lists, and then the GST portal reminding you that GSTR-3B is due. The good news: there are only three dates to remember this month. Which one is yours depends on how you file.

Tax ki tension kam karo: bas apni date pehchano, number check karo, aur time pe file karo.

The quick answer

Who you are Return period GSTR-3B due date
Monthly filer September 2026 Tuesday, 20 October 2026
QRMP filer, "22nd" group of states July–September 2026 (quarter) Thursday, 22 October 2026
QRMP filer, "24th" group of states July–September 2026 (quarter) Saturday, 24 October 2026

The GST portal's user guide says the monthly GSTR-3B is due on the 20th of the month after the tax period. For quarterly (QRMP) filers it is due on the 22nd or 24th of the month after the quarter, depending on the state or UT. The Government can extend these dates by notification. As of 15 Oct 2026 we did not find any extension for this cycle, but check the "Upcoming Due Dates" box on gst.gov.in before you file.

Monthly or QRMP: which one are you?

  • Monthly filer: you file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B every month. Larger businesses have to file monthly. Many small businesses also choose it because their clients want input tax credit (ITC) to show up quickly.
  • QRMP (Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment): small taxpayers can choose to file GSTR-3B once a quarter but still pay tax every month. For the first two months of a quarter you pay through challan PMT-06, usually by the 25th. In October you file one GSTR-3B for July, August and September together.

Not sure which one you chose? Log in to gst.gov.in and open Returns Dashboard. The portal shows your filing preference and the due date that applies to you.

Is my state in the 22nd group or the 24th group?

QRMP GSTR-3B is due on the 22nd of the month after the quarter for Chhattisgarh, MP, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Goa, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and the UTs of Daman & Diu and Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Puducherry, Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep. It is due on the 24th for Himachal, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, UP, Bihar, Sikkim, Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha, J&K, Ladakh, Chandigarh and Delhi (CGST Rule 61(1)(ii) table, inserted by Notification 82/2020-CT).

Simple rule: if you are unsure, file by the 22nd. Early is never late.

What happens if I miss the date?

Two separate costs can add up.

  1. Late fee. The GST portal guide says the late fee is worked out automatically as days of delay × ₹25 per day under CGST plus ₹25 per day under SGST (₹50/day in total). If your liability is nil, it is ₹10 + ₹10 (₹20/day). There is no late fee under IGST. The late fee has to be paid in cash, not from ITC.
  2. Interest. Under Section 50 of the CGST Act, tax paid late attracts interest from the day after the due date. The rate is set by notification, up to 18% a year. Interest applies to the tax you pay late in cash.

The penalty is not the only risk. A late GSTR-3B can hold up your next GSTR-1, delay ITC for your clients, and a long delay can lead to a notice. Not worth it.

5-minute pre-filing checklist

  1. Match sales to GSTR-1. Every invoice you raised for the period should be in GSTR-1. GSTR-3B should agree with it.
  2. Check GSTR-2B for ITC. Only claim credit that shows in your GSTR-2B and that you are actually entitled to. Leave out blocked credits.
  3. Reverse charge. Did you buy any service where you pay the GST, such as legal services from an advocate? Report it in table 3.1(d) and pay it in cash.
  4. Check your tax figure. Use our free GST calculator to work out GST on your taxable value, or to split a GST-inclusive amount into base and tax. It runs in your browser, with no signup and no data saved.
  5. Pay, then file. Make sure your cash ledger has enough for any amount ITC does not cover, then file with DSC or EVC.

Worked example: a QRMP filer's October return

Ravi, a QRMP filer in a "22nd" state, invoiced ₹1,00,000 + 18% GST in each of July, August and September. That is ₹54,000 of output GST for the quarter. His GSTR-2B shows ₹9,000 of eligible ITC across the three months. He paid ₹15,000 by PMT-06 for July and ₹15,000 for August.

  • Output GST for the quarter: ₹54,000
  • Less ITC: ₹9,000, leaving ₹45,000 net
  • Less PMT-06 payments already made: ₹30,000
  • Cash still to pay with the quarterly GSTR-3B: ₹15,000

The common mistake is to forget the PMT-06 payments and pay ₹45,000 again. The amount sits in your cash ledger, so it is not lost, but your money is stuck there for nothing.

Common GSTR-3B mistakes

  • Claiming ITC that is not in GSTR-2B.
  • Paying from the wrong head (CGST, SGST and IGST are separate).
  • Waiting until the last evening, when the portal is slowest.

FAQ

What is the GSTR-3B due date for September 2026? For monthly filers, GSTR-3B for September 2026 is due on 20 October 2026, per the GST portal rule of the 20th of the following month. Check gst.gov.in for any extension.

What is the GSTR-3B due date for QRMP filers in October 2026? QRMP filers file one GSTR-3B for July–September 2026, due on 22 or 24 October 2026 depending on the state of their principal place of business. Your portal dashboard shows your exact date.

What is the late fee for filing GSTR-3B late? According to the GST portal, ₹25 per day under CGST and ₹25 per day under SGST (₹50/day in total), or ₹10 + ₹10 per day for a nil return. There is no late fee under IGST. Interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act is charged on top of this for tax paid late.

Do QRMP filers pay tax every month? Yes. QRMP means quarterly returns with monthly payment. For the first two months of the quarter, tax is paid through challan PMT-06, and then one GSTR-3B is filed for the quarter.

Has the GSTR-3B due date been extended for October 2026? As of 15 October 2026 we found no extension notification for this cycle. Extensions are announced through CBIC notifications and on the GST portal, so check before you file.

Which states file quarterly GSTR-3B on the 22nd and which on the 24th? The 22nd applies to Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Goa, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and the UTs of Daman & Diu and Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Puducherry, Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep. The 24th applies to Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha, J&K, Ladakh, Chandigarh and Delhi (CGST Rule 61(1)(ii) table). Your portal dashboard shows your exact date.

Do QRMP filers need to count PMT-06 payments when filing quarterly GSTR-3B? Yes. Tax already paid through PMT-06 for the first two months of the quarter sits in the cash ledger and is used against the quarter's liability. Subtract it to avoid paying the same tax twice.
