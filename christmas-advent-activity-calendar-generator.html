How it works

  1. Enter the youngest and oldest child’s age, so every day suits the whole family.
  2. Choose your start date and whether you count down to Christmas Eve or Christmas Day.
  3. Tick what fits your home: quick weekdays, free only, outdoors, cooking.
  4. Press Make our calendar. Every day gets a different activity, and no two days in a row are the same kind (so you don’t get three crafts running). Day 1 starts with a paper-chain countdown, and the calendar ends with a calm Christmas Eve (and a Christmas Day idea if you count to the 25th).
  5. Swap or rewrite anything, then print it, cut out the cards or add it to your phone.

Tips for a calm, happy advent

  • Small beats big. Ten minutes of hot chocolate and a story is a lovely day. Children remember doing things with you, not how fancy they were.
  • Plan for tired days. Keep two or three “rescue” ideas handy, like pyjama morning or a story under a blanket, and swap one in when a day goes wrong. Missing a day is fine.
  • Put the long ones on weekends. Baking, a lights walk or a library trip fits a Saturday better than a school night. The quick-weekday option does this for you.
  • Let the kids own it. Ask them to open the card, read it out or tick the day off. Pre-readers can “read” the picture.
  • Keep it kind. Mix in a few giving days: a thank-you card for a neighbour, toys to donate, a call to a grandparent.
  • It works for every family. The ideas are about time together and winter fun. Edit any day to match your own traditions and faith.

Advent ideas by age

  • Toddlers and preschoolers (2 to 5): I spy with Christmas colours, freeze-dance to festive songs, a bubble-bath “snow” and story, sorting decorations by colour, handprint reindeer cards, cutting practice with child-safe scissors.
  • Kids 5 to 8: paper snowflakes, a treasure hunt with clues, salt-dough ornaments, a letter to Santa, a nature walk to collect five things, simple biscuits to decorate.
  • Kids 9 to 12 and teens: festive charades, a family playlist with its own cover art, growing sugar crystals, a time capsule, a board game night they choose, a secret good deed.

Advent calendar questions

Is the advent calendar generator free?

Yes. There is no signup, no email and no limit. You can make as many calendars as you like and print them at home.

Do you store anything I type?

No. The calendar is made by JavaScript in your own browser. Nothing is sent to a server or saved. Close the tab and it is gone.

What if my children are different ages?

Enter the youngest and oldest age. The tool picks activities that the youngest can join with a little help and that the oldest still enjoys. You can swap any day or type your own idea.

We are busy on school days. Will it still work?

Tick "Keep weekdays quick". Monday to Friday then get activities that take about 15 minutes, like a story under a blanket or a kind note, and the longer ones such as baking or a lights walk go on weekends.

Do I need to buy anything?

No. Tick "Free activities only" and every idea uses things most homes already have. Low-cost ideas, like baking or a park picnic, are only included if you want them.

How do I put it on my phone?

Press "Add to phone calendar (.ics)". It downloads a small calendar file. Open it and each day is added as an all-day event in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook.

Can I use it if we don't celebrate Christmas religiously?

Yes. The ideas are about time together, kindness and winter fun, so they suit families of any faith or none. Edit any day to fit your own traditions.
