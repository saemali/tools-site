Diwali Bonus & Festive Loan EMI: Calculate Before You Buy

Diwali falls on Sunday, 8 November 2026 (Lakshmi Puja, per Drik Panchang). The bonus has landed, or will soon. A phone, a bike, a new sofa and festive "0% EMI" offers are everywhere. Before you sign, spend five minutes on the maths.

Tax ki tension kam, EMI ki tension bhi kam: pehle calculate, phir celebrate.

The EMI formula (do not worry, the calculator does it)

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1]

  • P = loan amount
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = number of months

You do not need to do this by hand. Put any amount into our free EMI calculator and it shows the EMI, total interest and total repayment.

A worked example

Personal loan of ₹2,00,000 at 14% a year for 24 months: - r = 14 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.011667 - EMI ≈ ₹9,603 - Total paid ≈ ₹2,30,470, so interest ≈ ₹30,470

Same loan over 36 months: EMI ≈ ₹6,836, but interest ≈ ₹46,090. The smaller EMI costs you about ₹15,600 more.

(Illustrative rate only, not a quote from any lender. Your rate depends on your credit profile and the lender.)

"No-cost EMI": read the fine print

"No-cost" or "0%" EMI usually means one of these: - The interest is offered as an upfront discount, so you lose the cash discount you would have got by paying in full. - A processing fee is charged, often with GST on it. - If you miss a payment, late charges apply.

Compare: (price with no-cost EMI + fees) against (price if you pay in full with the best cash discount). If the second is lower, "no-cost" is not free.

Loan interest is exempt from GST (Notif. 12/2017-CT(Rate), entry 27). Processing and other fees are not covered by that exemption, so check your loan agreement's fee schedule for the GST charged on them.

Bonus first, loan second

A simple order that works for most households: 1. Clear expensive debt (credit card balances) with part of the bonus. 2. Top up your emergency fund. 3. Make a down payment. Every ₹10,000 you put down is ₹10,000 you do not pay interest on. 4. Borrow only the gap, for the shortest tenure whose EMI you can comfortably afford.

A common rule of thumb: keep all your EMIs together under about 30–40% of take-home pay. This is guidance, not a law, and lenders use their own limits.

Freelancers: plan for irregular income

If your income varies month to month: - Base the EMI on your lowest recent month, not your best one. - Set aside tax money first. If you are GST-registered, the GST you collected is not yours to spend (see our GST calculator). Also check your next advance tax instalment, due 15 December for most non-presumptive taxpayers. - If clients deduct TDS, your bank credit is smaller than your invoice. Use the TDS calculator to know your real net income.

Quick festive EMI checklist

  • [ ] EMI calculated for 2–3 tenures in the EMI calculator
  • [ ] Total interest + fees written down
  • [ ] Compared with the cash-discount price
  • [ ] EMI under your comfort limit even in a bad month
  • [ ] Emergency fund still intact

Prepaying later: a quick note

Plan to clear the loan early once next year's bonus arrives? Ask about foreclosure or part-prepayment charges before you sign. Some lenders charge a percentage of the outstanding amount, and that can cancel out the interest you would save. Use the EMI calculator to compare the interest left on the remaining tenure with the foreclosure fee quoted in your agreement.

Worked example: how much a down payment saves

You want a ₹1,20,000 purchase on a loan at 14% for 12 months (an illustrative rate):

Down payment Loan EMI (approx.) Total interest (approx.)
₹0 ₹1,20,000 ₹10,774 ₹9,293
₹40,000 ₹80,000 ₹7,183 ₹6,196

Using ₹40,000 of bonus as a down payment cuts the EMI by about ₹3,600 a month and saves about ₹3,100 in interest. Check your own numbers in the EMI calculator.

Common festive-loan mistakes

  • Comparing only EMIs, not total cost. A longer tenure always looks cheaper per month.
  • Stacking several small EMIs. Three ₹3,000 EMIs add up to one ₹9,000 commitment.
  • Ignoring fees such as processing and documentation charges.
  • Borrowing against a bonus you have not received yet.

Worked example: tenure vs total cost

The same ₹2,00,000 loan at an illustrative 14%, side by side:

Tenure EMI (approx.) Total interest (approx.)
24 months ₹9,603 ₹30,460
36 months ₹6,836 ₹46,080

Pick the shortest tenure whose EMI still fits in your worst month. If money gets tight later, you can usually talk to the lender, but a longer tenure costs you from day one.

Write it down before you sign

Note the loan amount, rate, tenure, EMI, every fee and the total payable on one page. If the sales counter cannot give you all six numbers, wait a day. The offer will usually still be there.

FAQ

How is EMI calculated? EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ [(1+r)^n − 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of months. A free EMI calculator does this instantly.

Is no-cost EMI really free? Often not entirely. The interest may be adjusted through a lost discount, and there may be processing fees with GST. Compare the total cost with the best price for paying in full.

Should I use my Diwali bonus to prepay a loan or buy with EMI? Generally, clear high-interest debt first, keep an emergency fund, use part of the bonus as a down payment, and borrow only what you need for the shortest tenure you can afford.

What is a safe EMI to income ratio? A common rule of thumb is to keep total EMIs under about 30–40% of take-home pay. This is guidance only, and lenders apply their own limits.
